# Lent content never outranks your own

Content arriving through a **Grant** composes at one fixed position beneath the
grantee's personal kit, so a name collision always resolves to the grantee's
own content and the lent contribution is recorded as shadowed in the plan and
the receipt. Accepting a grant therefore cannot change how someone's agent
already behaves, which makes accepting one safe without reading it first — the
property that lets grants circulate freely between coworkers. The consequence,
stated plainly because it is the reason to hesitate: a `team` **Scope** cannot
impose a standard this way. Team content is available, not binding, and a team
that wants a genuine standard states it as organization policy, which composes
as a non-overridable floor under ADR 0001 and is not a grant. That split is the
point rather than a gap: binding rules are reviewed policy, and lending is
convenience. Rejected: ranking lent content at its owning scope's position,
which makes team grants bind but lets accepting one silently change an agent's
behavior, and leaves a personal-to-personal grant with no natural position at
all. Also rejected: letting the granting edge carry its own position, which
hands the sender authority over the recipient's composition and turns every
grant into a decision the recipient must audit.
